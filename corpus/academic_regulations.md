# University Academic Regulations — Project Evaluation Corpus

> **Corpus status:** Synthetic evaluation material created for the Rulebook AI project.
> It is not an official regulation of RGPV or any other university.
>
> The two RGPV ordinance PDFs stored beside this file are preserved as external source
> documents. This synthetic corpus is deliberately written in a university-regulation
> style so that the application can be evaluated on a controlled, auditable corpus.

## 1. Scope and definitions

1.1 These regulations govern ordinary undergraduate academic administration for students
registered in a four-year degree programme. They describe admission, registration,
attendance, examinations, progression, academic standing, projects, results, appeals,
and related administrative processes.

1.2 “Academic year” means the period beginning with the first teaching semester and
ending after the associated summer examination and result-processing period. A
“semester” means an approved teaching and assessment period identified by the
academic calendar.

1.3 “Programme” means the degree programme in which a student is formally enrolled.
“Department” means the academic department responsible for that programme. “Dean”
means the Dean of Academic Affairs or an officer formally exercising that authority.

1.4 “Examination” includes an end-semester examination, a scheduled practical
examination, an approved project evaluation, or another assessment identified as a
university-level examination in the applicable scheme.

1.5 “Working day” means a day on which the university's administrative offices are
open, excluding declared university holidays. A deadline falling on a non-working day
moves to the next working day unless the notice establishing that deadline expressly
states otherwise.

1.6 These rules must be read together with the programme scheme, approved academic
calendar, examination notices, and any regulation expressly incorporated by reference.
Where a rule identifies a specific cohort, programme, or assessment, that scope controls
its application.

## 2. Admission and initial registration

2.1 Admission is subject to the eligibility conditions published for the relevant
programme and admission cycle. Meeting a minimum academic qualification does not by
itself guarantee admission because seats, merit, category requirements, and applicable
government directions may also govern the process.

2.2 A student admitted through a recognized diploma or lateral-entry route shall be
registered in the semester specified by the admission authority. The student's
programme record must identify the entry route so that progression and credit
requirements can be applied correctly.

2.3 At first registration, the student shall provide the documents required by the
admission office. Where an original document cannot immediately be produced, the
admission office may accept a verified undertaking for a limited period if its notice
permits that procedure.

2.4 A registration number shall be assigned after the admission record is verified.
Students must use the assigned registration number in examination forms, fee
transactions, project records, and formal academic correspondence.

2.5 Admission may be cancelled where material information supplied during admission is
found to be false or where a mandatory eligibility condition was not satisfied. Before
cancellation on the basis of disputed information, the student should be given an
opportunity to submit an explanation and supporting records unless a statutory direction
requires immediate action.

2.6 A student transferring between programmes must satisfy the conditions notified by
the university and the receiving department. Previously earned credits do not
automatically transfer. The receiving academic authority must identify which credits,
if any, are accepted toward the new programme.

2.7 A student may not simultaneously hold ordinary full-time registration in two
programmes of the university unless a specific university scheme expressly permits the
combination.

## 3. Academic calendar and teaching

3.1 The Academic Council shall approve the academic calendar for each academic year.
The calendar shall normally identify commencement of teaching, examination windows,
vacations, registration periods, result-processing milestones, and major administrative
deadlines.

3.2 Each semester should provide at least fourteen teaching weeks unless an approved
exception applies. Departments shall publish a timetable showing lectures, tutorials,
practical sessions, and other scheduled academic activities.

3.3 A change to the published timetable should be communicated through an official
departmental or university channel. Students are responsible for checking those
channels during the semester.

3.4 A department may arrange compensatory classes when teaching is lost because of an
institutional closure or an unavoidable academic disruption. The purpose of a
compensatory class is to preserve the approved teaching plan and it may be scheduled
outside the ordinary timetable when reasonable notice is provided.

3.5 Attendance is recorded separately for each course and for each attendance category
identified in the course scheme. A student's attendance percentage is calculated from
the sessions that are recorded as delivered and for which the student was expected to
attend.

3.6 Attendance records should be made available for review before the examination-form
deadline. A student who believes an entry is incorrect may submit a correction request
with supporting information.

3.7 Departments shall retain attendance records for the period prescribed for academic
records. Corrections must be supported by the class register, approved activity record,
or another reliable institutional source.

## 4. Attendance and attendance relief

4.1 A student appearing as a regular candidate for an end-semester examination shall
ordinarily have at least 75 percent attendance in the lectures and practical classes
prescribed separately for each course.

4.2 A shortfall may be considered for condonation only under the categories and
authority specified in the applicable attendance notice. Condonation is not an automatic
entitlement merely because a student's percentage is close to the threshold.

4.3 A request for attendance relief must normally identify the affected dates, the
course or courses concerned, the reason for absence, and the documentary evidence
available to support the request.

4.4 Medical circumstances must be supported by documentation issued or authenticated
through a recognized medical provider. The university may require additional
verification where the duration or circumstances of the absence make verification
reasonable.

4.5 Participation in an officially authorized university activity may be treated as
attendance for the affected academic activity where the competent authority has issued
the required authorization before or after the event.

4.6 Personal travel, ordinary social engagements, family functions, and private
appointments are not, by themselves, categories of guaranteed attendance relief.

4.7 A student whose attendance remains below the applicable requirement after all
permitted condonation has been considered shall not be treated as eligible solely on
the basis of an application that is still pending.

4.8 **Evaluation contradiction A — medical threshold.** Section 4.8 of this synthetic
document states that an approved medical exemption may be granted only when attendance
is at least **60 percent**. This rule is intentionally inconsistent with Section 9.3 of
the synthetic Attendance Administration Policy, which requires **65 percent** for the
same medical-exemption situation. The conflict is deliberate and is documented in
`docs/contradictions.md`.

## 5. Course registration and credits

5.1 Students shall register for the courses prescribed for their semester or for
approved backlog/improvement courses within the registration period.

5.2 A course shall carry the number of credits approved in the programme curriculum.
Credits are earned only after the student satisfies the passing requirements of the
course.

5.3 A student may be prevented from registering for a course where a prerequisite
course has not been passed, where the course is not offered, or where a programme
restriction applies.

5.4 Departments shall publish prerequisite relationships sufficiently before
registration. A prerequisite should not be inferred merely from the similarity of two
course names.

5.5 Students are responsible for checking their registered course list after the
registration window closes. An incorrect registration must be reported within the
correction period specified by the academic calendar.

5.6 A student who completes an approved elective but subsequently changes programme
may retain the elective credit only if the receiving programme accepts it as part of
the approved curriculum.

5.7 The minimum credits required for graduation are those stated in the approved
curriculum for the student's admission cohort. A student cannot substitute a higher
number of credits in one category for a missing mandatory course unless an approved
scheme expressly allows the substitution.

## 6. Examinations

6.1 University examinations shall be conducted according to the approved examination
schedule. The examination notice shall identify the date, time, venue or mode, and
courses included in the examination.

6.2 A student must complete the examination form and pay the applicable examination
fee within the notified period. Submission of a form without the required fee does not
automatically constitute a completed examination entry.

6.3 An admit card or examination authorization shall be issued after the examination
entry is verified. A student must report discrepancies promptly rather than assuming
that an incorrect entry will be corrected automatically.

6.4 A candidate shall comply with the instructions printed on the question paper,
admit card, and examination notice. Possession of prohibited material or unauthorized
electronic equipment may result in action under the examination misconduct procedure.

6.5 A student arriving after the permitted reporting period may be refused entry where
the examination instructions prescribe a fixed entry restriction.

6.6 A student who is absent from an examination is recorded as absent unless an
approved procedure provides a different result. The regulations do not create a
general right to a replacement examination for every reason for absence.

6.7 A medical emergency affecting an examination should be reported through the
procedure and within the time specified by the examination authority. Supporting
documentation may be required before a special consideration is granted.

6.8 A family wedding, ordinary travel difficulty, or personal scheduling conflict is
not expressly defined in this corpus as a qualifying ground for a replacement
examination.

6.9 Practical examinations may require attendance at a specified laboratory venue and
may include an external examiner where required by the approved scheme.

6.10 Project evaluations shall follow the approved project scheme. The scheme may
allocate marks between proposal, implementation, documentation, presentation, and
final evaluation.

## 7. Passing, grades, and academic standing

7.1 Each course shall have a published assessment scheme. The scheme may combine
continuous assessment, mid-semester assessment, laboratory work, assignments, and an
end-semester examination.

7.2 The minimum passing grade for a course is the grade specified in the approved
scheme applicable to the student's cohort.

7.3 A student who receives a failing grade in a course may be required to reappear in
the prescribed assessment or examination. The applicable examination notice shall
identify the available attempt.

7.4 A student's semester performance may be summarized through a Semester Grade Point
Average (SGPA), while cumulative performance may be summarized through a Cumulative
Grade Point Average (CGPA). The calculation shall use the approved credit weight of
each course.

7.5 The university may publish grade-point equivalents and division thresholds for
the applicable grading system. A student should not assume that a percentage-to-grade
conversion from an older regulation applies to a later admission cohort.

7.6 A candidate must satisfy all mandatory course and credit requirements before the
degree can be awarded. Passing a sufficient number of elective credits does not replace
a mandatory course unless substitution is formally approved.

7.7 **Evaluation contradiction B — improvement attempts.** Section 7.7 of this
synthetic document states that a student may improve results in **three** theory or
practical subjects after satisfying the other improvement conditions. This intentionally
conflicts with Section 6.4 of the synthetic Examination Administration Policy, which
states that improvement is permitted in **two** subjects. The conflict is deliberate and
documented in `docs/contradictions.md`.

7.8 Academic standing may be affected by failed courses, insufficient credits,
progression restrictions, or failure to satisfy programme-specific conditions. The
student portal may display a provisional status until official results are approved.

## 8. Progression and backlog

8.1 A student may progress to a higher semester only when the progression conditions
for the student's admission cohort and programme have been satisfied.

8.2 Where a rule specifies a maximum number of failed subjects or backlog credits,
the count shall be made according to the definitions in that rule. Theory and practical
components may be counted separately where the applicable rule says so.

8.3 A student who is provisionally permitted to attend a higher semester before the
qualifying result is declared does not acquire a permanent right to progression if the
qualifying result subsequently fails to satisfy the progression requirement.

8.4 A department shall publish the list of students whose progression is blocked or
conditional after official results are processed.

8.5 A student whose progression is blocked may use the notified appeal or review
procedure. Filing an appeal does not automatically suspend an academic deadline unless
the competent authority grants such relief.

8.6 A student carrying a backlog must register for the backlog assessment when the
course is offered and when the registration rules permit it. A backlog course is not
considered cleared merely because the student attended its classes again.

8.7 A student should consult the programme's approved curriculum before assuming that
a failed elective can be replaced by another elective. Replacement may require
departmental approval.

## 9. Academic projects and practical training

9.1 A final-year project shall be undertaken under the supervision arrangements
specified by the department. The department may assign a supervisor or approve a
student-proposed supervisor.

9.2 Project evaluation shall consider the components stated in the approved project
scheme. A project report submitted after the published deadline may be subject to the
late-submission rules.

9.3 Industrial training shall be completed for the duration and credit value approved
in the curriculum. Students must submit the required completion certificate and report.

9.4 A student may not claim attendance at an industrial training placement solely
because the placement was listed in an application. The placement must be verified
through the documents required by the department.

9.5 Project groups must maintain an auditable record of contributions where group
assessment is used. Individual performance may be considered where the scheme permits
individual differentiation.

9.6 Plagiarism, fabrication of project results, or submission of another student's work
may be treated as academic misconduct under the applicable procedure.

## 10. Results, revaluation, and scrutiny

10.1 Results are provisional until approved through the university's result process.
The publication of an online result does not prevent correction of a clerical or
processing error discovered through an authorized review.

10.2 A student may request scrutiny or revaluation only in the manner and within the
period announced for the relevant examination.

10.3 A revaluation request must identify the examination and course and must include
the prescribed fee where a fee is applicable. Requests submitted after the deadline
may be rejected without consideration on merits.

10.4 Revaluation does not guarantee an increase in marks. The final result is determined
according to the applicable revaluation procedure.

10.5 Where a tabulation error is established, the university may correct the result
through the authorized process. A correction should preserve an audit trail showing
the original and corrected entry.

10.6 Students should retain their grade sheets, fee receipts, examination forms, and
other records needed to establish their academic history.

## 11. Academic misconduct

11.1 Academic misconduct includes cheating, unauthorized collaboration, plagiarism,
fabrication of records, impersonation, and other conduct prohibited by the applicable
examination or academic-integrity procedure.

11.2 The response to alleged misconduct shall follow the prescribed process and may
include an opportunity for the student to provide an explanation.

11.3 Possession of a prohibited device in an examination hall may constitute misconduct
even if the student did not use the device, where the examination instructions
expressly prohibit possession.

11.4 A student should not publish confidential examination material or distribute
restricted assessment content through messaging services or public platforms.

11.5 Penalties shall be imposed only by the authority empowered under the applicable
procedure. A teaching assistant or student representative cannot independently impose
a university disciplinary penalty.

## 12. Scholarships and financial assistance

12.1 Scholarships administered by the university are subject to the eligibility,
documentation, and continuation requirements of the relevant scheme.

12.2 A scholarship may depend on academic performance, attendance, family income,
category, or other criteria expressly stated by the sponsoring authority.

12.3 A student receiving a scholarship must report a change that affects eligibility
when the scheme requires such reporting.

12.4 Scholarship applications shall be submitted through the notified portal or office
within the published application period.

12.5 A late scholarship application is not automatically accepted merely because the
student was unaware of the deadline. Any relaxation must come from the authority
empowered by the applicable scheme.

12.6 The university may verify scholarship documents with the issuing institution or
government database where permitted.

## 13. Hostel and residential conduct

13.1 Hostel accommodation is subject to room-allocation rules, capacity, eligibility,
and payment of the applicable charges.

13.2 Residents must comply with quiet hours, visitor requirements, safety instructions,
and reasonable directions of authorized hostel staff.

13.3 A resident shall not transfer an allotted room or bed to another student without
written permission.

13.4 Overnight absence from the hostel may require an entry or leave record where the
hostel handbook prescribes such a procedure.

13.5 Emergency maintenance issues should be reported through the designated hostel
channel. Students should not undertake electrical or structural alterations to hostel
property.

13.6 The hostel administration may issue notices about inspections, maintenance,
closures, and room changes. Such notices must identify the affected residents and the
period of the action where practicable.

## 14. Student records and official communication

14.1 The university's official email, student portal, notice board, or another channel
identified in an official notice may be used for academic communication.

14.2 Students are responsible for keeping their contact details current. A student
cannot ordinarily claim that a notice was invalid merely because a personal email
address was outdated when the university used the designated official channel.

14.3 Academic records should contain sufficient identifiers to distinguish students
with similar names.

14.4 Requests for correction of student records should identify the incorrect field and
provide supporting documentation.

14.5 The university may retain academic records for the period required by its records
policy or applicable law.

## 15. Appeals and administrative review

15.1 A student may appeal an academic administrative decision where the applicable
rule provides an appeal route.

15.2 An appeal should state the decision being challenged, the grounds for review, and
the remedy requested. Supporting documents should be attached where relevant.

15.3 An appeal against a decision does not automatically suspend the decision unless
the appeal authority grants interim relief.

15.4 The appeal authority may request records from the department, examination office,
or other responsible unit.

15.5 A decision issued on appeal should identify the authority making the decision and
the date on which it takes effect.

15.6 Where two provisions of this synthetic corpus are intentionally inconsistent, the
application must not silently choose one rule. It must expose the competing evidence
and label the result as contradictory unless an explicit scope or precedence rule
resolves the conflict.

## 16. Interpretation and precedence

16.1 Questions about the interpretation of these regulations should first be directed
to the office identified in the applicable notice.

16.2 A later rule does not automatically override an earlier rule unless the later
instrument expressly amends, replaces, or supersedes the earlier provision.

16.3 A programme-specific provision applies to that programme when its scope is clear.
A general provision applies where no more specific provision governs the same matter.

16.4 A rule concerning an identified admission cohort must not be applied to a different
cohort without evidence that the rule was made applicable to that cohort.

16.5 If the available corpus contains two rules that both clearly apply to the same
student, same situation, and same time period but prescribe incompatible outcomes, the
system should identify the conflict rather than manufacture a reconciliation.

## 17. Graduation and degree award

17.1 A degree shall be awarded only after the student has satisfied the approved
curriculum, credit, examination, and other mandatory programme requirements.

17.2 The final academic record shall reflect the student's approved results and
graduation status.

17.3 A student with an unresolved mandatory course, required credit, or progression
condition is not eligible for final degree award until that condition is cleared.

17.4 The university may issue a provisional certificate where the applicable procedure
permits it and all stated conditions for issuance are satisfied.

17.5 Students should verify their final course and credit audit before the graduation
processing deadline.

## 18. General student responsibilities

18.1 Students are expected to read official notices affecting their academic status and
to meet published deadlines.

18.2 A student should retain copies of applications and receipts for matters where
proof of submission may later be required.

18.3 No employee or student representative may promise an outcome that requires approval
from another competent authority.

18.4 Informal advice from classmates, teaching staff, or administrative staff should
not be treated as an official amendment to a regulation.

18.5 Where this corpus is silent on a specific circumstance, the application must say
that the available rules do not establish an answer. It must not invent a university
policy from common practice at other institutions.

## 19. Synthetic conflict notice

19.1 This corpus intentionally contains exactly three evaluation contradictions. They
are not accidental errors in the dataset. They exist to test whether a retrieval and
reasoning system can surface incompatible rules instead of producing a confident,
unsupported answer.

19.2 The contradiction register identifies the source file, section, competing
provision, and intended test question for each planted conflict.

19.3 Apart from those registered conflicts, differences between the two official RGPV
ordinance PDFs should be treated as source-version differences unless the documents
themselves establish that the provisions apply simultaneously to the same population
and period.

## 20. Corpus-use notice

20.1 The synthetic rules in this file are intended only for software evaluation. They
must not be represented to a student as official university policy.

20.2 The application should display the corpus identity and source metadata whenever it
answers a question. The purpose is to make the provenance of an answer visible.

20.3 When an answer depends on a specific provision, the application should expose the
section and source location used to support the claim.

20.4 When evidence is insufficient, the application should distinguish “not covered”
from “the system failed to retrieve evidence.” Evaluation logs should preserve enough
information to investigate such failures.

20.5 The final evaluation should report the measured performance of the implemented
system rather than claiming perfect accuracy without testing.
## 21. Registration corrections and academic status

21.1 During the registration correction period, a student may request removal of an
incorrectly added course, addition of an eligible course, or correction of a course
code where the registration record does not match the approved registration.

21.2 A correction request must be supported by the student's registration receipt,
department approval, or another record accepted by the academic office.

21.3 A department may reject a requested change when the change would create a
prerequisite violation, exceed an approved credit load, or occur after the correction
period without an authorized exception.

21.4 Students should check whether a requested correction changes their examination
eligibility, fee liability, attendance record, or graduation audit.

21.5 A course appearing on a student's timetable does not by itself prove that the
course was formally registered. The official registration record is the controlling
academic record.

21.6 Where a student believes two official systems display different registration
information, the student should report the discrepancy to the designated academic
office. The system should not infer which record is correct solely from a screenshot.

## 22. Examination scheduling and postponement

22.1 The examination office shall publish the examination timetable through an official
channel. A timetable may be revised when an administrative, institutional, or
unavoidable operational circumstance requires a change.

22.2 When an examination is rescheduled, the revised notice supersedes the earlier
timetable for the examination identified in the notice, unless the notice states that
the earlier schedule remains valid.

22.3 Students are expected to verify revised schedules rather than rely exclusively on
a timetable downloaded before a revision.

22.4 A postponement of one examination does not automatically postpone other
examinations in the same semester.

22.5 The examination office may arrange different sessions for courses with approved
special requirements. A student must attend the session assigned to the student's
registration record unless an authorized change is granted.

22.6 The corpus does not specify a universal number of days of notice that must precede
every examination timetable change. Where a user asks for such a number, the system
must not invent one.

22.7 If an examination is cancelled after students have arrived at the venue, the
applicable notice should state the next action, if one has been determined. This corpus
does not prescribe a fixed compensation amount for travel or other personal expenses.

## 23. Student support and academic advising

23.1 Each department should provide an academic advising mechanism through which
students can discuss course registration, prerequisites, progression, and graduation
requirements.

23.2 Academic advising is intended to assist students in understanding applicable
rules. Advice from an adviser does not amend a regulation unless the adviser is the
authority empowered to issue the relevant decision.

23.3 Students approaching graduation should review their credit audit before the final
registration period. A credit audit may identify mandatory courses, elective
requirements, outstanding backlogs, or other graduation conditions.

23.4 Where a student's programme record appears inconsistent with the approved
curriculum, the student should request a formal review rather than relying on an
informal calculation.

23.5 Departments may conduct orientation sessions explaining examination procedures,
registration deadlines, attendance requirements, and academic integrity. Attendance
at an orientation session does not itself alter a student's academic obligations.

23.6 A student who misses an advising session remains responsible for published
deadlines and official notices.

23.7 The university may provide academic support, remedial instruction, mentoring, or
other assistance. The availability of support does not by itself change the minimum
passing requirement of a course.

## 24. Data, auditability, and source provenance

24.1 Academic decisions should be supported by records sufficient to identify the
student, the applicable programme or cohort, the relevant academic period, and the
authority responsible for the decision.

24.2 When a regulation is cited in an academic decision, the decision should identify
the provision relied upon where practicable.

24.3 A copy of an unofficial message, social-media post, or verbal statement should
not be treated as an authoritative amendment unless the message itself was issued
through an authorized official channel and clearly identifies its authority and scope.

24.4 When two documents appear to address the same subject, the application should
retain the source identity and document date so that users can inspect the context.

24.5 A source document may contain tables, headings, footnotes, amendments, or
cross-references that affect interpretation. Retrieval should preserve enough context
for a reader to inspect the relevant passage.

24.6 If a user asks a question requiring information absent from the corpus, the
application should state that the available material does not establish the answer.
It should not substitute a general answer from model knowledge.

24.7 If a retrieved passage contains an explicit exception, the application should
include the exception in the evidence presented with the answer rather than quoting
only the general rule.

24.8 If two applicable passages prescribe different numerical thresholds, dates,
eligibility conditions, or outcomes and no precedence rule resolves them, the
application should report the conflict explicitly.

24.9 Evaluation logs should preserve the question, retrieved evidence identifiers,
classification, and final response so that an incorrect result can be investigated.

24.10 The purpose of provenance is not merely to display citations. It is to allow a
student or evaluator to move from a generated claim back to the underlying source and
judge whether the claim is supported.
